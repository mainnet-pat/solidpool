# Audit of the BN254 Groth16 verifier for the pools (bn-native-sound, 3 runtime inputs, composed) — 2026-11-13

> Provenance: an independent audit by a Claude agent on the Fable model, commissioned by the project
> owner on 2026-11-13. The auditor worked read-only, without access to earlier reviews' conclusions.
> This is the auditor's report as returned, saved by the coordinating session at the owner's request.
> It is an AI audit, not a human one. The scripts, logs, test key and patched generator copy it cites
> were kept outside this repository, in `/Code/tmp/claude-0/verifier-audit-2026-11-13/` on the machine
> that ran it; "the audit folder" below means that directory.

Audited:
- `groth16_composed` at `4475ba8` (branch `pool-composed`);
- the template `bch-next/service/verifier/verifier-artifact-3in.json` (code sha256 `37c8b9ec…82b6`, 8,212 B, redeem 69 B);
- its use in `web/src/verifier.ts` and `web/src/pool-client.ts`;
- `withdraw` in `BchPool.scash` and `TokenPool.scash`.

Judge: stock libauth 3.1.0-next.8 from `bch-next/node_modules`, `createVirtualMachineBch2026(true)` and `(false)`.

## Verdict

**I found no way to make the verifier accept a statement the circuit does not prove, pay anyone but the proven recipient, spend a nullifier twice through an encoding alias, or take, shrink or freeze the verifier's coins.** Every finding below is Low or Informational. Nothing here blocks mainnet on soundness grounds.

The conclusion rests on five things, each reproducible from the audit folder:

1. **The key is Tornado's.** All 28 numbers of `Verifier.sol` equal upstream `tornadocash/tornado-core` master (fetched on the day), `withdraw_verification_key.json`, the proving key's α/β/δ, and `tornado-bn254-3in.json`. The IC points are used in Tornado's order (`logs/vk-check.log`, 43 comparisons, 0 differences).
2. **Every baked constant was recomputed without the generator's helpers** and found as a push in the deployed bytes (`logs/constants-check.log`, 146 lines, 0 failures):
   - the 88 δ slopes (as the three `K_DS` hashes) and the three δ start states;
   - the 36 GLS table coefficients and `Rconst`;
   - η, ω, γ1, b′, the ψ constants, p, r, the binding offset, the schedules and `TAG`.
3. **The equation is Groth16's for arbitrary inputs, not only for valid proofs.** For four random false statements, the product of every factor the identities describe final-exponentiates to exactly `e(−A,B)·e(C,δ)·e(α,β)·e(vk_x,γ)`, computed by noble with the key parsed from `Verifier.sol` (same log, section 6).
4. **The deployed template is the program the red-team suite ran on.** It equals a test-key build instruction for instruction (4,485 identical). The only differences are 67 data pushes: the VK-derived constants of point 2 plus `K_code` (`logs/same-shape.log`). The suite's 161 rejections and 3 token cases reproduce on that build (`test-key/redteam.log`).
5. **The attacks the suites do not try were tried on the production key with real SDK-built withdrawals**, with the witness rebuilt as the strongest prover would. All were refused, in the place the design says (`logs/exp-forge.log`, `exp-g2.log`, `exp-alias.log`, `exp-rules.log`, `exp-outside.log`).

The two largest residual risks: BCHN itself was not run (its binaries here lack shared libraries), and the bytecode's equivalence to the stated formulas is established by tests and structure, not by proof.

## Findings, ranked

### L1 (Low, liveness, client): the client spends any plain coin at the verifier lock, including unconfirmed coins a stranger made

- **Where:**
  - `bch-next/web/src/pool-client.ts:78` (`spendableCoins`: no token, ≥ 1,000 sat, sorted by outpoint) and `:334` (first three);
  - `web/src/provider.ts:51-52` (unconfirmed coins are kept).
- **Evidence (argument from the code; not run):**
  - Anyone can pay coins to a pool's verifier lock and grind a txid that sorts first.
  - Such coins are valid verifier coins, so the withdrawal verifies.
  - If their parent is unconfirmed and its maker double-spends it at miners, every withdrawal built on them is dropped when the conflict confirms. So is every pool transaction chained after it on the same server.
  - The attack costs fees only and repeats.
- **Impact:** withdrawals seen through the targeted server fail until the next block. No funds move.
- **Fix:** prefer confirmed coins, then coins created by a transaction that spent the pool's master. Use a stranger's unconfirmed coin only when nothing else exists.

### L2 (Low): a third party can rebuild a withdrawal without changing its effect

- **Where:**
  - `script.mjs:84` (`FILL`: the lengths of `FILL_0` and `FILL_1` are committed nowhere);
  - Groth16 itself;
  - the pool's `withdraw` (no rule on inputs before the verifier's three or outputs before the recreated three).
- **Evidence (`logs/exp-rules.log`, `logs/exp-forge.log`):** stock libauth accepts each of these in both modes:
  - `FILL_0` or `FILL_1` one byte longer, or `FILL_0` ten bytes shorter;
  - a re-randomised proof `(sA, B/s, C)` with the witness rebuilt, no secret needed;
  - an anyone-can-spend input inserted just before the verifier's inputs, with or without an extra output;
  - an extra output inserted before the recreated coins (it is paid from the miner fee).
- **Refused:** any change to `FILL_2`, the statement, the recipient, the salt or the payout output; an input after the verifier; an output after the recreated coins; an unlocking of 10,001 B.
- **Impact:**
  - The txid is not stable before confirmation, so nothing may be chained on an unconfirmed withdrawal by txid.
  - Whoever wins the relay race can keep the fee above the relay minimum: about 1,700 sat on the BCH pool (39,800 paid, 38,134 B) and 1,000 sat on the token pool.
  - The payout cannot be redirected.
- **Fix:** if txid stability matters, commit `|FILL_0|` and `|FILL_1|` (for example inside `ZT`) or fix their lengths, and have `withdraw` pin its input and output counts. Otherwise document it and never key on a withdrawal's txid.

### L3 (Low): the op budget is sized with libauth, which charges less than BCHN

- **Where:** `transaction.mjs:33-46` (`sizeFills`, run by `web/src/verifier.ts:46`). libauth in both `groth16_cashscript/node_modules` and `bch-next/node_modules` is stock; the repository's `patches/@bitauth__libauth@3.1.0-next.8.patch` is applied to neither.
- **Evidence:**
  - BCHN charges an `OP_DEFINE`d body as pushed bytes (`bitcoin-cash-node/src/script/interpreter.cpp`, `metrics.TallyPushOp(it->second.size())`).
  - Stock libauth charges 0 (`logs/define-cost.log`: difference 0 for 100 B and 1,000 B bodies).
  - Every input defines 16,313 B of bodies, so BCHN counts 16,313 more per input.
- **Impact:** none today. The 6.0% margin the prover targets is 5.8% on BCHN (`logs/exp-liveness.log`). The generator README's statement that its figures are "BCHN accounting" is not true of this checkout.
- **Fix:** apply the patch where the client sizes fillers, or add 16,313 to each measured cost. Keep the margin.

### L4 (Low): the verifier's coins can be spent outside a withdrawal

- **Where:** `script.mjs:258` (brand: input 0's token category and capability only) and `:88-96` (`GRAPH`).
- **Evidence (`logs/exp-outside.log`):**
  - `bch.finish`, `bch.finish2` and `token.finish`, each with a withdrawal's three verifier inputs and outputs appended: consensus accepts. Standard refuses only the size (117,920 B).
  - Beside a minting NFT of the pool's category at an anyone-can-spend lock: accepted in both modes.
  - Refused: the three coins alone; beside another category's minting NFT; beside a mutable or immutable NFT of the pool's category.
- **Impact:** none on funds.
  - The coins come back with the same lock and value.
  - A valid proof is still needed, though for any root: the verifier does not know the pool's roots.
  - The comment at `script.mjs:19-21` ("a verifier coin can only be spent beside the pool contract that reads the statement") is stronger than the rule.
  - The brand is as strong as the pool's guarantee that only its master ever holds a minting NFT of its category.
- **Fix:** correct the comment. No code change needed.

### L5 (Low): the test suites never isolate the last comparison with a consistent false witness

See "What the existing tests miss". The missing experiments were run here and all refused. The scripts can be added to the suite as they stand.

### I1 (Informational): documents that no longer describe the build

- `bn-native-sound/README.md` describes the two-input, three-inputs-only build. Its claims against the code:

  | README | code at `4475ba8` |

### I2 (Informational): `brandOf` does not check the category's length

At `web/src/verifier.ts:16-21`, `code.set(brand, artifact.brandOffset)` writes whatever length `hexToBin(category)` gives. A longer string would overwrite the instructions after the brand push. `engine.ts:447` filters pool categories to 64 hex digits, so this is not reachable today. Add `if (brand.length !== 33) throw`.

### I3 (Informational, outside the verifier): pool rules noticed while tracing the statement

Not audited; listed for the owner.
- `RECENT_ROOTS = 8`: eight deposit-and-finish pairs retire a root, so a proof can be made stale for about 1.4M sat of fees per rotation.
- The nullifier tree is keyed by 64 bits of `sha256(nullifierHash)`. Two notes that collide there cannot both be withdrawn. Targeting one needs 2^64 work and the victim's nullifier hash, which is private until it is spent.

## What I verified as correct, and how

### 1. The verification equation

- **Design, re-derived.**
  - Fp12 = Fp[X]/(X¹²−18X⁶+82). A class in Fp12*/Fp6* is a monic degree-6 polynomial.
  - Each chunk identity `D_k = P_k(X)·S_{k+1}(−X) − P_k(−X)·S_{k+1}(X) ≡ 0 (mod M)` says `[P_k] = [S_{k+1}]`.
  - Chained, `[f′] = [c]^λ` with λ = 6x+2+p−p²+p³ = m·r, so `FE(f′) = 1`.
  - The final exponentiation kills Fp6*, so only the class of each constant matters.
  - The check is `Σρ^(33−k)·D_k(z) = z·q(z²)·M(z)` at a Fiat–Shamir `z`.
- **Miller loop and lines.** NAF of 6x+2 (65 events, leading digit implicit); affine line `y_P − λx_P·w + (λx_T − y_T)·w³`; terminal additions of ψ(B) and −ψ²(B). Checked numerically two ways:
  - constants-check section 6 (arbitrary statements);
  - the four captured withdrawals, whose statements are true under noble's pairing with Tornado's seven IC points and zeros for relayer, fee and refund (`logs/accepted-statements.log`).
- **Frobenius.** All checked against noble's field arithmetic directly:
  - `Frob²(w) = ηw`, `Frob⁶(w) = −w`, `Frob(w) = Φ(γ1)·w`;
  - ψ = (conj(x)γ1², conj(y)γ1³) is the untwisted p-Frobenius;
  - −ψ² = (ωx, y), −ψ³ = (ω·ψ_x, ψ_y);
  - ψ(Q) = [p]Q on G2.
- **GT constants.**
  - `FE(R[k][0]) = e(IC_{k+1},γ)` and `FE(R[k][1]) = e(IC_{k+1},γ)^p`.
  - The `ηX` substitutions give the p² and p³ powers.
  - `FE(Rconst) = e(α,β)·e(IC0,γ)·Π_k e(IC_k,γ)^(−2^64(1+p+p²+p³))`.
- **The last comparison** (`script.mjs:377-396`): `(R − z·acc·M(z)) mod p`, then `OP_NOT` is the script's result. In every forged run below, this is the only thing that refuses.

### 2. Witness-supplied values

| value | pinned by | if false |
|---|---|---|
| inverse norms `t` (88 + 2), in `S_i` | `t < p` and `t·2N(y) ≡ 1` / `t·N(x−x_Q) ≡ 1` (`kernels.mjs:38,49`; `script.mjs:156-157`) | the solution is unique; `+p`, sign-bit alias and wrong value are refused in the loop |
| 33 states, in `S_i` | hash-committed in `d_i` before ρ; `< p` | free, but a false one fails the last comparison (`exp-alias.log`, "FALSE STATE", roles 0, 1, 2) |
| residue root `s^c`, in `STMT` | committed in `d_2`; `< p` | free; any value is sound |
| nibbles `N_0..N_2`, in `STMT` | mask `≤ 0x0f` in every role; exact integer `acc_k − 2^64Σπ − n_k ≡ 0 (mod r)` in role 2 | several decompositions are valid (lattice shifts); all bind the same `n_k` |
| quotient `WQ` | inside `z`'s preimage; length ≡ 0 mod 32, 32..5,376 B; leading word ≠ 0; every word `< p` | fails the last comparison |
| `HAND_1`, `HAND_2` | byte equality with the canonical encoding of what the previous role computed; decoded `< p` | refused by the previous role |
| `DG` | each third recomputed by the input that hosts its preimage | refused |
| `DS_i`, code pieces, redeem | SHA-256 against `K_DS[i]` and `K_code`; P2SH32 | refused |
| `FILL_2` | all zero, ≤ 10,000; its push is in `z`'s preimage | changes `z` |
| `FILL_0`, `FILL_1` | all zero, ≤ 10,000; **length unconstrained** | L2 |

No value is unconstrained in a way that reaches the accept decision.

### 3. Input validation

- **A, C on the curve; B on the twist:** role 0 (`script.mjs:275-281`). G1 has cofactor 1.
- **B in G2:**
  - The walk is the exact affine group law; an exceptional step has no `t`.
  - The endpoint `T == −ψ³(B)` gives `λ(ψ)B = O`. With `gcd(Norm(λ(ψ)), 2p−r) = 1` and `r ∤ 2p−r` (both recomputed), B has order r.
  - Five points outside G2 were refused at the endpoint guard with roles 0 and 1 accepting (`logs/exp-g2.log`). They include a point of order 10069 and the honest B plus a cofactor point.
- **Infinity:** not encodable (affine words; (0,0) is off both curves).
- **Canonical encodings:** every word is `BIN2NUM(w ‖ 0x00)` and `< p` (or `< r`). On the production key, with every commitment recomputed, 49 of 49 aliases were refused by a decoding or canonical-form guard (`logs/exp-alias.log`), so one nullifier hash has one 32-byte spelling. The 49:
  - `+r`, `+2r`, bit 255 and the sign-bit alias of root, nullifier hash and payee;
  - `+p` and sign-bit aliases of six coordinates, two `s^c` words, a `t` and a state in each role, and three quotient words;
  - nibble `0x80`;
  - leading-zero and signed-zero quotient words.
- **31-byte recipient:**
  - The payee word is `sha256(lock ‖ salt)[0:31] ‖ 0x00`, below 2^248 < r, so it is always a canonical field element.
  - Redirecting needs a second preimage of a 248-bit hash.
  - The 4-byte salt is the fixed tail of the preimage, so `lock ‖ salt` parses one way.
- **Folding six inputs to three:** `vk_x = IC0 + n0·IC1 + n1·IC2 + n2·IC3` is Tornado's `vk_x` at relayer = fee = refund = 0. Accepting means Groth16 holds for `[root, nullifierHash, payee, 0, 0, 0]` under the unchanged key, a statement of the unchanged circuit. Soundness is Groth16's.

### 4. The verifying key

- `extract-vk.ts` output equals `Verifier.sol` (the auditor's own parser), in Tornado's input order. G2 coordinates are swapped correctly (Solidity writes the imaginary part first).
- `web/src/prover.ts` pins the circuit and proving key by SHA-256; both equal the files in `tornado-core/build/circuits`.
- **Branding cannot alter the key.** The brand is one 33-byte push in role 2 (`00 ce 21 <33 B> 88`), present once. `kcodeOffset` is the 32-byte push in the redeem. The captured verifier lock is reproduced from the template and the pool's category (`logs/exp-forge.log`, first line).

### 5. The three-input composition

- **Roles.** Role = input index − (input count − 3), required in [0,3).
  - Role 0: events 0–23, curve checks.
  - Role 1: events 24–47.
  - Role 2: events 48–64, terminal, endpoint, binding, quotient, brand.
- **Between inputs.** Every role reads `DG`, `STMT` and the z-tail from the last input's bytes and derives ρ and z itself. Role i reads `HAND_{i+1}` from input i+1 and requires it to equal its own result.
- **Code loading.** The redeem takes fixed ranges of the unlockings at count−3 and count−2, requires `SHA256 == K_code`, then defines and runs. A flipped code byte is refused in every input.
- **Each input alone.** Roles 0 and 1 accept any consistent witness; they never see the last comparison. This is by design: `GRAPH` requires all three locks to be equal, so role 2 always runs.
- **Decoy.** The pool compares the last input's locking bytecode with its `verifierLock`, and that lock runs role 2. An input after the verifier, a fourth verifier coin, and swapped verifier inputs were all refused.

### 6. Statement binding

- Offset 357 = 2 (push header of `DG`) + 96 + 3 (header of `STMT`) + 256.
- Role 2 pins `DEPTH == 7`, both sizes, and the raw unlocking length as the sum of minimal push lengths. Any non-minimal push or extra byte fails even without the consensus rule.
- libauth's consensus VM refuses non-minimal pushes, non-push opcodes and a surplus stack item (`logs/exp-rules.log`, first block).
- In BCHN the flags are `MINIMALDATA` (mandatory), `SIGPUSHONLY` and `CLEANSTACK` (`validation.cpp:1498-1504`).
- On the real withdrawals, all refused: `DG` pushed with `PUSHDATA2` (alone, and with `FILL_2` shortened to keep the size), a prepended `OP_0`, a prepended `OP_NOP`.
- Bytes 357..453 equal `STMT[256..352)` in both captured withdrawals.

### 7. Limits and liveness (`logs/exp-liveness.log`, production code, 18 statements)

| | input 0 | input 1 | input 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| unlocking with no filler | 9,674 B | 9,676 B | 5,178 B + 32·n_q |
| op cost, stock libauth, standard | 7.04M–7.52M | 6.99M–7.48M | 5.71M–7.47M |
| worst headroom at the 10,000 B cap, BCHN accounting | 6.2% | 6.7% | 6.7% (n_q 150) |

- Inputs 0 and 1 do not depend on the quotient. Their worst case is every nibble nonzero.
- Input 2 holds 150 words (9,978 B). Its margin grows with n_q, so the size cap binds before the op budget.
- Over 20,000 random statements, 1 in 645 needs more than 150 words and 1 in 30 more than the client's 141.
- With root and nullifier fixed, at least 59% of salts fit (worst of 40 notes). A payee contributing no bits always fits, because the two-input maximum is 132 words. So no note is stuck for all salts except with negligible probability, and a new root changes the statement anyway.
- Fees (estimates): the largest withdrawal the client makes stays under the prepaid fee at 1 sat/B: about 38.4 kB of 39,800 on BCH, about 39.8 kB of 40,600 on the token pool.
- An unlocking of exactly 10,000 B is accepted; 10,001 is not.

### 8. The verifier's coins

- Each of the last three outputs must carry the same lock, the same value and no token as the input it recreates.
- On both withdrawals, 15 cases each were all refused: value ±1, a fungible token, a pool-category NFT, another lock.
- A verifier coin of 5,000 sat is recreated at 5,000 or refused.
- A token on the spent coin is not preserved; it belonged to whoever sent it.
- The coins cannot be removed by anyone, the owner included.

## What the existing tests miss

- **`tamper.ts`** flips one bit and recommits nothing. Every case dies at a hash, size or handover check; no arithmetic guard and never the last comparison is reached. Its composition cases are sound but omit the verifier beside `finish`, a second minting NFT, an input inserted before the verifier, and different coin values.
- **`run-real-proof.ts`** tampers the fixture and calls the honest prover, which throws before the VM runs.
- **`redteam.mjs`** (test key) recommits digests and challenges but takes the handovers from the honest values.
  - 45 of its runs stop in the post-loop section of role 0 or 1, where the handover is compared. They include `−A`, `−C`, `−B`, "A from proof 1", every role-0 and role-1 state tamper, and the residue root.
  - The last comparison is reached only by role-2 state and quotient tampers.
  - It never changes a statement together with its nibbles, so the binding guard catches every public-input run before the pairing is asked anything.
- **None of them** runs on the production key with recomputed commitments.

Tried in this audit on the production key, real withdrawals, stock libauth:

| experiment | result |
|---|---|
| Payee changed to the attacker; nibbles, states, digests, ρ, z, handovers and floor quotient rebuilt; pool arguments and payout changed | refused: pool input and roles 0, 1 accept, role 2's last comparison is false |
| Nullifier hash + 1; root := 0; A := 2A; C := −C; B := 2B, all rebuilt the same way | refused at role 2's last comparison only |
| A false state in role 0 (first and last), role 1 and role 2, with handovers following the false value and the quotient rebuilt | refused at role 2's last comparison only |
| B outside G2, five kinds | refused at role 2's endpoint guard only |
| 49 encoding aliases | refused by a decoding guard |
| Re-randomised proof | **accepted** (L2) |
| Filler, graph, coin, recipient and salt changes | as listed in L2, L4 and section 8 |

- **The pool's hashing.** That the on-chain MiMC tree equals the circuit's is shown only by the four captured withdrawals being true statements.
- **SolidCash, websnark, the browser bundle and any deployed mainnet instance** were not examined.
- **Test-key acceptances** were used only to reproduce the red-team suite. No conclusion about the production key rests on them.

## Resolution, 2026-11-13

Added by the coordinating session after the audit; the auditor did not write this section.
The verifier's code is unchanged (sha256 `37c8b9ec…82b6`), and no contract or contract artifact changed.

| Finding | What was done | Where | Tested by |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Fixed as recommended, and for the hashing coins too. A confirmed coin comes first. Next comes one whose transaction's output 0 is the master holding the pool's minting NFT, read by txid and checked against its hash. A stranger's unconfirmed coin is used only when the others are too few. | `web/src/pool-client.ts` (`coinsAt`, `madeByThePool`) | `test/app-flows.ts`: a stranger's coin that sorts first, at either lock; four ordering checks against a stub provider |
